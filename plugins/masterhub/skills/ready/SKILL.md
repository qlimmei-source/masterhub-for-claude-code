# Ready: what the result looks like

A person often has a general idea but cannot picture every menu in advance, and then edits it live, one small thing at a time. That is slow and tiring. This skill gives them what they may not know: examples, words, decisions, checks. They decide; you lead.

Creative direction is their taste and their references. Execution, technique, look and convenience are your job, at the highest level.

## When

- Before any new thing where look or behaviour matters: a page, a proposal, a presentation, an interface, a document for other people, a video.
- When the user says "I don't know how it should be", "just a general idea", or is making a third small edit in one block.
- Not for a small edit from a ready list.

## 1. The idea, in one or two lines

In the user's words: what it is, for whom, what the reader or viewer should do afterwards. If that is unclear, ask one question. No questionnaire.

## 2. What the user may not know: brief, before the draft

- **Examples.** 2–3 of the best in the world in this genre, by name, each with one line "what to take from it". A concrete device, not "inspiration".
- **Words.** 3–6 terms that make this thing easy to steer, each explained in a line: "tooltip: a hint on hover", "accordion: a block that unfolds", "grid", "white space", "hierarchy". A new word goes into the project's glossary.
- **Decisions.** Only those that change the result, 2–4 of them, with options, your recommendation first and why it is better. Where the difference has to be seen, show it: a sketch, a screenshot, a small page with the options side by side.

## 3. A draft on one screen

The most important piece, quickly and at a high level. It sets the bar for all the rest. The user looks and says what they like and what they don't. This costs far less than editing the whole thing.

## 4. The "Done" card: after the user answers, before the big work

One short card, in `08_AGENTS/HUB/` or next to the task:

- **Seen first:** what a person sees in the first 3 seconds.
- **Parts and behaviour:** each block in one line; what is open, what is folded, what happens on hover and on click; the phone.
- **Left out:** what is deliberately not being made.
- **Do not touch:** everything the user called good. No later edit breaks this list.
- **The bar:** nothing jumps or overlaps, it reads on a phone, each number has a source, the language follows the plain-language rules.
- **How it will be checked:** a script (measurements, clicks, shifts), screenshots at 1440 and 390 px, the council for text.

The card goes to the helper in full, as the task.

## 5. During edits

- Praise from the user goes straight into "Do not touch". An edit goes to the hub word for word; if it could recur, it becomes a rule in the project's `CLAUDE.md`.
- Before each link to the user: compare with the card and run the checks. Failed: back to the helper, without showing the user.
- Now and then, in one line: a hint to the user, such as a word, a device, a question they may not have asked. Not a lecture.

## 6. Keep the bar, offer more

- Big things start from a one-screen plan: the parts, and one sentence of "why" for each. Each part answers the question the previous one left.
- The best 2–3 first parts are the standard. Each next part is compared with them. At the end, compare the last three with the first three, and bring anything weaker up to the bar.
- At the start or the end of a big task, offer 1–2 concrete things beyond the order, in one line each: a tested library, a tool, a device, and what it would give. Do them only on "yes". Fetch and install nothing without "yes".
