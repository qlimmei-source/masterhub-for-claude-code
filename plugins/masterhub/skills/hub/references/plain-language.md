# Plain language

Write for a person, not for a robot. Read this before any text that other people will read. Replies to the user follow it too.

## The main rules

1. **The reader was not in the room.** They read it once and have no questions left. No internal labels, codes or abbreviations.
2. **Say it the way a person would say it aloud.** Read the sentence aloud; if nobody talks like that, rewrite it.
3. **The simplest exact word.** "Start" and not "commencement". Before each word, ask whether a simpler and more exact one exists.
4. **One thought, one short sentence.** A colon that glues two thoughts together becomes two sentences.
5. **Only one way to read it.** Every word like "result", "program" or "package" says whose and of what.
6. **Say what will happen and who does it**, not the name of an action.
7. **Say what is there and what gets done.** Keep facts visible, without a wall of "no" and "cannot".
8. **The main thing comes first and largest.** Then what it consists of. The background goes smaller and later.
9. **Each number says what it counts and where it comes from.** A number you cannot explain in one sentence does not belong there.
10. **Every detail comes from a source.** No source, no sentence. A guess is marked as a guess: "I think, because…".
11. **Someone else's words stay theirs.** Quote them exactly: "Anna writes: '…'". An unclear word gets a question, not your own meaning.
12. **Order follows the reader's questions.** Each section answers the question the previous one left. Steps go in the order they are done.
13. **Conditions go into a footnote.** The sentence reads clean the first time; the fine print sits under a star.
14. **A confident, adult tone.** A proposal is a project, not a trial. No "let's try" and no "for a start".
15. **Name the action, not the tool.** "Rewrote the report in plain words", not "written with AI".
16. **Professional terms over household words.** A term a reader may not know gets a short explanation, one that shows the work behind it.
17. **Each sentence earns its place.** Ask what it explains right here. No answer: remove it.
18. **What the user says "for you" stays with you.** Their tastes, jokes and ways of working are instructions, not content for the reader.

## Adding to this list

When the user corrects your language (a word, a tone, an order), add the lesson to the project's `CLAUDE.md`, section `{#language}`, the same hour: before → after, and a one-line check. If a similar rule is there, add the example to it.

## Check before showing

Read the text aloud in your head. Would a person say this to another person? If not, it goes back for rewriting.
